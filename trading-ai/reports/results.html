What the model is

A gradient-boosted ensemble ranks roughly — liquid USDT perpetual contracts every four hours and holds a dollar-neutral, factor-neutral long/short book. It does not forecast the direction of crypto; it forecasts which contracts will out- or under-perform their peers over the next few hours, from ~110 features spanning vol-normalised and residual momentum, taker order-flow imbalance, trade-intensity microstructure, funding and open-interest positioning, and market-state conditioning.

Training is expanding-window walk-forward, retrained quarterly, with a purge and a 48-bar embargo before every test window. Fills are at the next bar's open and pay exchange fees, half the spread, and square-root impact in the participation rate against that bar's actual dollar volume; funding is settled every eight hours on realised rates; delisted contracts are force-liquidated with penalty costs.

What would break this

  • Fill assumptions. The cost model charges a quarter of the flow at maker fees. A book that actually crosses the spread on every order pays the conservative column, not the base one.
  • Capacity. The edge lives in the mid-cap half of the universe. The capacity table shows where the participation cap starts eating the return; past that point the strategy is a different, worse strategy.
  • Leverage availability. The operating point assumes the venue carries the stated gross notional across ~100 contracts at once, through a liquidation cascade, without raising margin requirements. Exchanges raise them exactly when you least want it.
  • Crowding. Short-horizon cross-sectional reversal in crypto is not a secret. The IC-by-year table is the thing to watch: if the edge is being competed away, it shows up there first.
  • Regime dependence. The sample contains one full bull/bear cycle. It does not contain a sustained low-volatility, low-funding crypto market, because one has not happened yet on perpetuals.
